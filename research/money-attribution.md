# Money layer and attribution / false-flag layer

Research notes for the explainer site. They cover how the mechanisms work conceptually and how investigators break them. They are not an operational guide. Research date: 2026-11-05. "UNVERIFIED" marks a claim we could not confirm against a primary source or two independent sources.

## TL;DR

- Bitcoin is **pseudonymous, not anonymous**. Every transaction is public forever, and simple heuristics can collapse millions of addresses into a few million "entities" ([Meiklejohn et al. 2013](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~smeiklejohn/files/imc13.pdf)).
- Mixers, bridges and privacy coins add noise, but the **cash-out point** is where people get caught: a KYC exchange, an OTC desk, a stablecoin issuer that can freeze funds. Chainalysis says over 50% of illicit funds still end up at centralized exchanges ([Unchained, Jul 2024](https://unchainedcrypto.com/50-of-illicit-funds-end-up-at-centralized-crypto-exchanges-chainalysis/)).
- Attribution never rests on one artifact. Analysts combine several weak signals (infrastructure, code, TTPs, operator mistakes). Sophisticated actors therefore forge exactly the signals analysts look for (Olympic Destroyer's Rich header, Turla's use of OilRig infrastructure).
- Operator **OPSEC slips** (a single non-VPN login, a reused address, a misconfigured server) expose people far more often than cryptography fails.

## 1. Bitcoin pseudonymity and clustering

**Mechanism.** Every Bitcoin transaction spends earlier outputs ("inputs") and creates new outputs. All of it sits on a public ledger. An address is a pseudonym. The goal of analysis is to group pseudonyms into entities.

- **Common-input-ownership heuristic.** If several addresses are inputs to the same transaction, assume one entity controls them, because spending requires every private key ([Meiklejohn et al. 2013, IMC](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~smeiklejohn/files/imc13.pdf)).
- **Change-address heuristic.** A one-time output address in a multi-output transaction is probably the sender's "change". Meiklejohn et al. call this an idiom of use, not a protocol property. Applied naively it is unsafe: it produced a giant false super-cluster that included Mt. Gox. The refined version found 3,540,831 change addresses with an estimated 0.17% false-positive rate ([paper](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~smeiklejohn/files/imc13.pdf)). Their dataset (April 2013) held more than 16M transactions and 12M public keys, which collapsed to about 3.3M clusters ([same](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~smeiklejohn/files/imc13.pdf)).
- **Peel chains.** A large balance moves through a long chain of transactions. Each hop "peels" a small payment to a recipient and sends the change onward. Once change is identified, an analyst can walk the chain hop by hop and read off each peel's recipient (often an exchange) ([Meiklejohn et al.](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~smeiklejohn/files/imc13.pdf)).
- **Why KYC exchanges are the chokepoint.** Clustering tells you that entity X sent coins to Coinbase. The exchange's KYC records then tell you who X is. In Welcome to Video, investigators clustered 221 addresses and asked exchanges who owned the accounts that cashed out. Coinbase identified the operator, Jong Woo Son ([Security Boulevard](https://securityboulevard.com/2019/10/welcome-to-video-raid-leads-to-337-arrests-due-to-bitcoin-exchanges-that-use-strong-kyc/); [DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/south-korean-national-and-hundreds-others-charged-worldwide-takedown-largest-darknet-child)).
- **Courts now accept the method.** In *US v. Sterlingov*, the court admitted Chainalysis Reactor evidence as "the product of reliable principles and methods". Reactor had attributed more than 900,000 addresses to Bitcoin Fog. The court also noted that the case did not depend primarily on Reactor ([opinion via FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-dis-crt-dis-col/115886148.html)).

## 2. Mixers, CoinJoin and smart-contract mixers

**Three architectures, explained conceptually:**

| Type | How it hides links | Trust and weak point |
|---|---|---|
| Centralized (custodial) mixer | You send coins in and the operator returns *different* coins from a pool | You trust the operator. The operator and its logs are a single point of seizure |
| CoinJoin | Many users co-sign **one** transaction with equal-sized outputs, which breaks the common-input heuristic | Non-custodial, but a **coordinator** server organizes the rounds and can be shut down. Amounts and timing can still leak |
| Smart-contract mixer (Tornado Cash) | Deposit to a contract, then later withdraw to a fresh address with a zero-knowledge proof of "I deposited something" | No operator holds the funds, but relayers, the front end, and users' deposit/withdraw patterns are observable |

**Enforcement timeline (numbers):**

- **Blender.io.** On 6 May 2022 it became the first mixer OFAC ever sanctioned. Lazarus used it for more than $20.5M ([Treasury jy0768](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy0768); cited in [jy1933](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy1933)).
- **Tornado Cash.** OFAC designated it on 8 Aug 2022, saying it had laundered more than $7B since 2019, including over $455M stolen by Lazarus ([Treasury jy0916](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy0916); [Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/tornado-cash-ofac-designation-sanctions/)). It was re-designated in Nov 2022. On 26 Nov 2024 the Fifth Circuit held that immutable smart contracts are not "property". OFAC **delisted** Tornado Cash on 21 Mar 2025 but kept developer Roman Semenov sanctioned ([Treasury release mirror](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/dprk/2025/dprk-250321-treasury01.htm); [Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/international-compliance-blog/treasury-department-delists-tornado-cash-following-the-fifth-circuits-decision.html)).
- **Roman Storm trial.** On 6 Aug 2025 an SDNY jury convicted him on **one** count, conspiracy to operate an unlicensed money-transmitting business. It hung on money-laundering conspiracy and sanctions conspiracy. DOJ sought a retrial. On 25 Aug 2026 Judge Failla moved the retrial to **26 Apr 2027** while a Rule 29 acquittal motion is pending ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-08-26-tornado-cash-roman-storm-retrial-april-2027-412761); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/roman-storm-retrial-delayed-april-2027)). Court filings show Chainalysis itself ran a Tornado relayer in 2022 ([same](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-08-26-tornado-cash-roman-storm-retrial-april-2027-412761)).
- **Samourai Wallet (Whirlpool CoinJoin).** Founders Keonne Rodriguez and William Lonergan Hill were arrested on 24 Apr 2024. They pleaded guilty in Jul 2025 to unlicensed money transmission conspiracy, and the laundering count was dismissed. They were sentenced in Nov 2025 to **5 years (Rodriguez)** and **4 years (Hill)**. Prosecutors alleged more than $237M in criminal proceeds went through the service ([IRS-CI](https://www.irs.gov/compliance/criminal-investigation/founders-of-samourai-wallet-cryptocurrency-mixing-service-sentenced-to-five-and-four-years-in-prison)). The Record reverses the two terms, which is probably an error ([The Record](https://therecord.media/samourai-wallet-crypto-mixer-founders-sentenced)).
- **Wasabi.** Days after the Samourai arrests, zkSNACKs announced its coordinator would shut down. It did so on 1 Jun 2024. The wallet now uses community coordinators ([Wasabi on X](https://x.com/wasabiwallet/status/1790046826369781859?lang=en); [Atlas21](https://atlas21.com/coinjoin-comparison-of-tools-to-improve-privacy-on-bitcoin/)). Teaching point: CoinJoin is non-custodial, but **the coordinator is still a legal chokepoint**.
- **Bitcoin Fog** (2011-2021). It processed about 1.28M BTC, worth about $400M at the time. Roman Sterlingov was convicted by a jury on 12 Mar 2024 and sentenced on 8 Nov 2024 to **150 months**. He also forfeited about 1,345 BTC ([IRS-CI](https://www.irs.gov/zh-hans/compliance/criminal-investigation/jury-finds-russian-swedish-operator-of-bitcoin-fog-guilty-of-running-the-darknet-cryptocurrency-mixer); [Willkie](https://complianceconcourse.willkie.com/articles/bitcoin-fog-operator-sentenced-to-twelve-years-in-prison-for-money-laundering-and-related-charges/)). He maintains he was only a user ([Infosecurity](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/man-12-years-running-crypto-mixer/)).
- **ChipMixer.** Taken down 15 Mar 2023 by the FBI, Germany's BKA and Europol. Authorities seized 1,909.4 BTC (about $46M) and 7 TB of data. Europol says it may have laundered **152,000 BTC** (about EUR 2.73B). DOJ says more than $3B, including over $700M tied to stolen funds and over $17M to ransomware ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/chipmixer-platform-seized-for-laundering-ransomware-payments-drug-sales/); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/europol-seizes-46m-from-cryptocurrency-mixer-after-2-88b-laundered)).
- **Sinbad.io.** Sanctioned and seized 29 Nov 2023 as Lazarus's "key money-laundering tool". Industry experts consider it the successor to Blender. It laundered part of the Axie/Ronin and Horizon Bridge proceeds ([Treasury jy1933](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy1933)). Chainalysis reported that mixer inflows from illicit addresses fell to $504.3M in 2023 and partly credits Sinbad's shutdown ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2024/02/15/crypto-money-laundering-dropped-30-last-year-chainalysis-says)).

## 3. Chain hopping, bridges and privacy coins

**Chain hopping.** Value moves across assets and chains (for example ETH to BTC) through bridges, decentralized swap protocols, or no-KYC instant-swap services. Each hop forces the analyst to correlate two ledgers. That correlation is often possible from amounts and timing, but it costs effort. Chainalysis saw bridge use for laundering grow in 2023, driven largely by Lazarus ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2024/02/15/crypto-money-laundering-dropped-30-last-year-chainalysis-says)). In the Bybit laundering, roughly 361k ETH was swapped to BTC via THORChain ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bybit-hacker-launders-1-billion-stolen-funds)). See section 5.

**Monero.** Three mechanisms protect privacy. **Ring signatures** hide which of several decoy outputs is really being spent. **Stealth addresses** give each payment a one-time destination. **RingCT** hides amounts. Limits:

- Möser et al. (PoPETs 2018) found that 64.04% of inputs in early Monero had **zero mixins**, so the real input was obvious. Those exposed inputs then "chain-reaction" eliminated decoys elsewhere, which made about 62% of inputs with 1+ mixins deducible. A "guess-newest" heuristic picked the real input about 80% of the time. These findings mostly concern pre-RingCT history. Monero later mandated minimum mixins and RingCT ([arXiv 1704.04299](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04299); [PoPETs](https://petsymposium.org/popets/2018/popets-2018-0025.php)).
- Teaching point: the cryptography can be sound while **usage patterns and historical defaults** leak information.

**Zcash.** Transparent "t-addresses" work like Bitcoin. A **shielded pool** (z-addresses, zk-SNARKs) hides sender, receiver and amount. Kappos, Yousaf, Maller and Meiklejohn (USENIX Security 2018) showed that simple heuristics can "shrink its anonymity set considerably". Most shielded activity was transparent-in/transparent-out by miners and founders, and round-trip deposit/withdraw amounts were linkable ([arXiv 1805.03180](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03180); [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity18/presentation/kappos)). UNVERIFIED: the specific figure that about 65-70% of withdrawals were linked (recalled from the paper, not re-checked).

**Delistings (regulatory chokepoint).** OKX delisted XMR and ZEC on 5 Jan 2024. Binance delisted XMR on 20 Feb 2024. Kraken delisted XMR across the EEA on 31 Oct 2024, citing "regulatory changes" ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/276176/binance-delisting-monero); [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/crypto/markets/kraken-delists-monero-in-europe-triggering-price-dip); [Blofin summary](https://blofin.com/en/academy/education/monero/monero-and-regulation)). Teaching point: a privacy coin that is hard to trace is also **hard to cash out** at a regulated venue, so users are pushed back toward a swap or OTC hop.

## 4. Cash-out: where anonymity usually ends

- **Nested exchanges and OTC brokers.** A "nested" service runs on top of a bigger exchange's accounts, so the big exchange never sees the end customer. **Suex** (21 Sep 2021) was the first exchange OFAC sanctioned. OFAC said over 40% of its known history was illicit and that it served at least 8 ransomware variants. **Chatex** (Nov 2021) was sanctioned for using Suex as a nested exchange ([TRM](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/treasury-designates-cryptocurrency-exchange-chatex); [OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/us-authorities-target-ransomware-sanction-another-crypto-exchange)). Sources disagree on Chatex's exact date (7-9 Nov).
- **Garantex.** OFAC sanctioned it in Apr 2022 ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/a7a5-grinex-russian-crypto-economy-ofac-sanctions-august-2025/)). It was taken down on 6-7 Mar 2025 by the US Secret Service with Germany and Finland. Authorities seized its domains, and more than $26M was frozen. DOJ says it processed at least $96B since 2019. Aleksej Besciokov was arrested in India ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/us-sanctions-grinex-crypto-exchange-garantexs-successor/); [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/cybercrime/russian-exchange-garantex-reborn-grinex/)). Within weeks a look-alike, **Grinex**, appeared, and OFAC designated it as the successor on 14 Aug 2025 ([TRM](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/grinex-emerges-as-likely-garantex-rebrand); [Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/a7a5-grinex-russian-crypto-economy-ofac-sanctions-august-2025/)). Teaching point: takedowns are whack-a-mole, but each rebrand leaves on-chain continuity that analysts can follow.
- **Stablecoin freezes.** Tether can blacklist addresses. Just before the Garantex takedown it blocked about $28M in USDT on Garantex wallets ([Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/cybercrime/russian-exchange-garantex-reborn-grinex/)). BlockSec counts $1.26B frozen across 4,163 addresses in 2025 ([BlockSec](https://blocksec.com/blog/1-26-billion-frozen-usdt-blacklisting-on-ethereum-and-tron-in-2025)). The T3 FCU (Tether, TRON, TRM) reports freezing more than $300M by Oct 2025, including about $9M from the Bybit hack ([TRM](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/t3-financial-crime-unit-marks-enforcement-victory-100-million-in-criminal-assets-frozen-across-five-continents); secondary: [Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/tether-t3-fcu-unit-freezes-450-million/)). Teaching point: a **centralized issuer** is a privacy-hostile point inside "crypto".
- **Money mules, gift cards and prepaid cards.** Mules move illicit money on someone else's behalf, sometimes without knowing it. A red flag is deposits from unrelated parties followed by fast conversion to crypto or prepaid cards ([FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/common-frauds-and-scams); [FDIC OIG](https://www.fdicoig.gov/alerts/public-service-alert-money-mules)). The mule takes on the KYC exposure, which is the whole point for the criminal and also why mules get prosecuted.
- **Chinese-language OTC / "underground banking".** Chainalysis reports that DPRK reliance on Chinese-language money movement and guarantee services rose 355% to over 1,000% in 2025 ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/crypto-hacking-stolen-funds-2026/)). TRM calls this network the "Chinese Laundromat" ([TRM](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/north-korea-and-the-industrialization-of-cryptocurrency-theft)).

## 5. Famous tracing wins (and partial wins)

- **Bitfinex 2016 to arrests in 2022.** 119,754 BTC were stolen. Ilya Lichtenstein and Heather Morgan were arrested 8 Feb 2022. **More than 94,000 BTC** (about $3.6B) sat in the original receiving wallet, and agents seized it after getting into files that held the private keys. About 25,000 BTC had gone through a "complicated laundering process" ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/two-arrested-alleged-conspiracy-launder-45-billion-stolen-cryptocurrency)). Lichtenstein later admitted he was the hacker ([DOJ plea](https://justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/bitfinex-hacker-and-wife-plead-guilty-money-laundering-conspiracy-involving-billions)). UNVERIFIED (secondary only): sentenced Nov 2024 to 5 years (Lichtenstein) and 18 months (Morgan). Plea coverage: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/08/03/new-york-man-admits-being-original-bitfinex-hacker-during-guilty-plea-in-dc-to-bitcoin-money-laundering.html). Lesson: stolen funds are **frozen in plain sight**. Spending them is the risky step.
- **Welcome to Video (2019).** The site received at least 420 BTC across 7,300 transactions. IRS-CI tracing combined with exchange KYC located the server and the admin. 337 users in at least 18 countries were charged ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/south-korean-national-and-hundreds-others-charged-worldwide-takedown-largest-darknet-child)).
- **Colonial Pipeline (2021).** Colonial paid about 75 BTC. On 7 Jun 2021 DOJ seized **63.7 BTC** (about $2.3M), probably the affiliate's share, after tracing it to an address whose key the FBI had obtained ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-seizes-23-million-cryptocurrency-paid-ransomware-extortionists-darkside); [Evertas](https://evertas.com/news/fbi-recovers-85-of-btc-paid-to-hackers-in-wake-of-colonial-pipeline-ransomware-attack-additional-seizures-to-follow/)). DOJ did not disclose how the key was obtained.
- **Ronin bridge (Mar 2022).** 173,600 ETH and 25.5M USDC were stolen, often cited as about $620M. The FBI attributed it to Lazarus/APT38 on 14 Apr 2022, and OFAC added the receiving ETH address to Lazarus's SDN entry the same day ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/fbi-links-largest-crypto-hack-ever-to-north-korean-hackers/); [TRM](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/us-authorities-tie-north-koreas-lazarus-group-to-ronin-bridge-hack-through-ofac-sanctions)). More than $455M later went through Tornado Cash ([Treasury jy0916](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy0916)).
- **Bybit (21 Feb 2025, about $1.5B).** The FBI attributed it to DPRK "TraderTraitor" within 5 days and published laundering addresses. It noted that the actors were "proceeding rapidly", converting to BTC "dispersed across thousands of addresses on multiple blockchains" ([FBI IC3 PSA](https://www.ic3.gov/psa/2025/psa250226)). All of the roughly 499k ETH was laundered in about **10 days** (22 Feb to 3 Mar), mainly via THORChain. Bybit's CEO said about 77% remained traceable ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bybit-hacker-launders-1-billion-stolen-funds); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2025/03/04/thorchain-sees-record-usd4-6b-volume-after-bybit-s-usd1-4b-hack)). Chainalysis describes a roughly **45-day** DPRK laundering cycle overall and $2.02B stolen by DPRK in 2025 ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/crypto-hacking-stolen-funds-2026/)). Lesson: **traceable does not mean recoverable**. Speed plus a permissionless swap protocol outran freezes.
- UNVERIFIED / very recent: Nov 2026 reports say the FBI attributed a "$6B+ Binance theft" to TraderTraitor ([The Diplomat](https://thediplomat.com/2026/11/6b-binance-heist-exposes-how-north-korea-turned-hacking-into-a-state-revenue-stream)). Confirm before using.

## 6. Attribution and false flags

**How analysts attribute.**

- **The Diamond Model** (Caltagirone, Pendergast and Betz, 2013). Every intrusion event links **adversary, capability, infrastructure and victim**. Analysts "pivot" from a known feature to discover unknown ones, for example from a C2 IP to other malware that uses it ([DTIC PDF](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA586960.pdf); [Recorded Future](https://www.recordedfuture.com/blog/diamond-model-intrusion-analysis)).
- **Evidence classes.** Code reuse and compiler artifacts, infrastructure overlap, TTPs, language and timezone artifacts, victimology, and operator mistakes. The FBI's Sony 2014 case combined malware links to known DPRK tools, hard-coded IPs that communicated with known DPRK infrastructure, and similarity to the 2013 South Korea attacks ([DOJ/FBI update](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/update-sony-investigation)). Comey later added that "Guardians of Peace" sometimes "got sloppy" and posted from DPRK-exclusive IPs without a proxy ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/storyline/sony-hack/sony-hackers-got-sloppy-used-north-korean-ips-fbi-director-n281556)). Some firms argued at the time that the text read as Russian-authored ([same](https://www.nbcnews.com/storyline/sony-hack/sony-hackers-got-sloppy-used-north-korean-ips-fbi-director-n281556)). This is a good example of contested language evidence.

**How actors mislead (with sourced cases):**

- **Olympic Destroyer (PyeongChang, Feb 2018).** The wiper carried a **Rich header byte-identical to a Lazarus/Bluenoroff sample**. Kaspersky showed it was forged: the header claimed a Visual Studio 6 build, yet referenced `mscoree.dll`, which did not exist then ([Securelist, 8 Mar 2018](https://securelist.com/the-devils-in-the-rich-header/84348/)). In Oct 2020 the US indicted six GRU Unit 74455 (Sandworm) officers. DOJ said they "took steps to pin the Olympic Destroyer attack on North Korea" ([DOJ remarks mirror](https://www.globalsecurity.org/intell/library/news/2020/intell-201019-doj02.htm); [Help Net](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2020/10/20/sandworm-hackers/)). Lesson: **a forged signal fails when it is internally inconsistent** with the rest of the artifact.
- **Guccifer 2.0 (2016).** A "lone Romanian hacker" persona who showed poor Romanian ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/03/22/more-evidence-ties-guccifer-2-0-to-russian-intelligence/)). The Daily Beast reported (22 Mar 2018) that the operator once **failed to activate the VPN** and left a Moscow IP in a US social-media company's logs, which investigators tied to a GRU officer ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/03/22/more-evidence-ties-guccifer-2-0-to-russian-intelligence/); [BankInfoSecurity](https://www.bankinfosecurity.com/report-guccifer-20-unmasked-at-last-a-10737)). Sourcing caveat: this rests on an anonymous source to one outlet. The **official** attribution is the 13 Jul 2018 Mueller indictment of 12 GRU officers ([Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guccifer_2.0)). Treat the VPN-slip detail as "reported", not proven in court.
- **Turla hijacking OilRig (NSA/NCSC, 21 Oct 2019).** Russia's Turla acquired the Iranian group's Neuron/Nautilus tools and operational data and took over some victims the Iranian group had already compromised. The Iranian operators were "almost certainly not aware" ([CISA alert](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2019/10/21/nsa-and-ncsc-release-joint-advisory-turla-group-activity); [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/russian-hackers-use-iranian-threat-groups-tools-servers-as-cover/)). This is an example of **"4th-party collection"**: riding on another actor's access. It means tool or infrastructure overlap alone can point to the wrong country.
- **Vault 7 "Marble" (WikiLeaks, 31 Mar 2017).** CIA string-obfuscation source code (676 files), with test examples in Chinese, Russian, Korean, Arabic and Farsi. Its stated purpose was to stop analysts linking malware to a developer through unique string obfuscation ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2017/03/31/wikileaks-exposes-cia-anti-forensics-tool-that-makes-uncle-sam-seem-fluent-in-enemy-tongues/567104); [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/57586/intelligence/vault7-marble-framework.html)). Caveat: it shows a **capability**, not proof of any specific false-flag operation. "Proof the CIA framed Russia" headlines overreach.
- **Cloud Atlas / Inception (2014).** Kaspersky's VB2016 paper documents planted strings: Arabic in the BlackBerry implant, Hindi in the Android implant, a "JohnClerk" project path in the iOS implant, Chinese-style malware served to researcher machines, and C2 traffic through compromised South Korean home routers. All were judged deliberate misdirection ([VB2016 paper](https://www.virusbulletin.com/virusbulletin/2016/11/vb2016-paper-wave-your-false-flags-deception-tactics-muddying-attribution-targeted-attacks/); [PDF](https://media.kasperskycontenthub.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2017/10/20114955/Bartholomew-GuerreroSaade-VB2016.pdf)).
- **Timestamp forgery.** PE compile timestamps are trivially editable. Kaspersky compared a sample's timestamp with its upload time to judge legitimacy ([Securelist](https://securelist.com/the-devils-in-the-rich-header/84348/)). In general, analysts use timestamps as statistical "working hours" signals across many samples, never as single proof (inference, not from a single source).

## 7. Ransomware groups' anonymity model and how it broke

**Model.** Ransomware-as-a-service splits roles. Core developers run the locker, the panel, the **Tor onion leak site** and a **negotiation portal**. Affiliates break in and deploy, and payouts are split. Members use handles only. The design means no single affiliate can name everyone, while the brand stays persistent.

- **Conti leaks (27 Feb 2022).** After Conti backed Russia's invasion, @ContiLeaks published 60,694 internal chat messages (Jan 2021 to Feb 2022), source code and Bitcoin addresses. Ransomwhere added 239 addresses worth about $13.1M ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/conti-ransomwares-internal-chats-leaked-after-siding-with-russia/); [The Record](https://therecord.media/conti-ransomware-gang-chats-leaked-by-pro-ukraine-member)). Lesson: **insiders** are a bigger risk to the group than cryptography.
- **REvil.** Kaseya attacker Yaroslav Vasinskyi was arrested in Poland in Nov 2021 and sentenced in the US on 1 May 2024 to 13 years 7 months ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/revil-ransomware-member-extradited-to-us-to-stand-trial-for-kaseya-attack/); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaseya_VSA_ransomware_attack)). OFAC also sanctioned him with Chatex ([OCCRP](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/us-authorities-target-ransomware-sanction-another-crypto-exchange)). Russia's FSB arrested 14 people on 14 Jan 2022. Four were later sentenced in Russia, and four were freed on time served in Jun 2025 ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/russia-arrests-revil-ransomware-gang-members-seize-66-million/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/06/24/four_revil_ransomware_suspects_time_served/)).
- **LockBit / Operation Cronos.** Announced 20 Feb 2024. The NCA-led task force took 34 servers, seized the leak site and affiliate panel, froze 200 crypto accounts, and reused LockBit's own leak site to publish its findings ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2024/02/lockbit-ransomware-operation-shut-down.html); [Intel 471](https://www.intel471.com/blog/what-lies-ahead-after-lockbits-disruption)). On 7 May 2024 the US, UK and Australia named **LockBitSupp as Dmitry Yuryevich Khoroshev**, sanctioned him, unsealed a 26-count indictment and offered up to $10M. Cronos identified 194 affiliates ([NCA](https://nca-newsroom.prgloo.com/news/op-cronos); [Infosecurity](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/lockbit-leader-identity-revealed/)). Khoroshev remains at large in Russia ([case library](https://cybercaselibrary.com/cases/lockbit-ransomware-takedown/)). Lesson: seizing the **backend** exposes affiliate data, and naming the leader damages the brand's trust even without an arrest.

## Diagram ideas

1. **"The funnel"**: many dirty addresses, then mixer/bridge/swap noise, then a **narrow neck** at a KYC exchange, OTC desk or stablecoin issuer, then a real name. Annotate the neck with Coinbase/WTV, Tether/Garantex and Bitfinex.
2. **Clustering animation**: show a transaction with 3 inputs merging into one colored entity (common-input), then a 2-output transaction where one output is tagged "change". Animate a peel chain dripping small outputs into exchange icons.
3. **Mixer architecture triptych**: custodial pool vs. CoinJoin single transaction (equal outputs plus coordinator) vs. smart contract (deposit, zk proof, withdraw). Mark each one's "seizable point" in red (operator / coordinator / front-end and relayer).
4. **Monero ring**: one real input hidden among decoys. Then show "chain-reaction" elimination as zero-mixin rings knock out decoys elsewhere.
5. **Bybit timeline**: day 0 theft, day 5 FBI attribution, day 10 all ETH swapped to BTC, about day 45 the cycle continues. Show the gap between "traceable" and "recovered".
6. **Diamond Model with pivot arrows**. Then overlay forged signals (fake Rich header on the capability vertex, hijacked OilRig C2 on the infrastructure vertex) to show where false flags attack the model.
7. **Olympic Destroyer "spot the forgery"**: a Rich header table claiming VS6 next to an import of `mscoree.dll` (anachronism highlight).
8. **RaaS org chart**: core, affiliates, leak site, negotiation portal, payment. Mark break points at the Conti insider leak, the Cronos backend seizure and the Khoroshev naming.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Bitcoin is anonymous."** It is pseudonymous. The full history is public, and clustering has been documented since 2013 ([Meiklejohn et al.](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~smeiklejohn/files/imc13.pdf)).
- **"A mixer makes funds untraceable."** Mixers add uncertainty, but operators get seized along with their logs (ChipMixer, Bitcoin Fog), and inflows and outflows can be correlated statistically. Courts have accepted such tracing ([Sterlingov opinion](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-dis-crt-dis-col/115886148.html)).
- **"Tornado Cash was delisted, so it's legal now / Storm was acquitted."** OFAC delisting is separate from criminal law. Storm was convicted on one count and faces retrial on the others ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-08-26-tornado-cash-roman-storm-retrial-april-2027-412761)).
- **"Monero/Zcash are perfectly private."** Research shows leaks from usage patterns and historical defaults ([Möser et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04299); [Kappos et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03180)), and cash-out venues are shrinking.
- **"If it's traceable, it gets recovered."** Bybit was traced in real time, and most of it was still converted within about 10 days ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bybit-hacker-launders-1-billion-stolen-funds)).
- **"Attribution = one smoking-gun artifact."** Single artifacts (language strings, Rich headers, IPs, compile times) are exactly what gets forged. Attribution is cumulative and probabilistic ([Securelist](https://securelist.com/the-devils-in-the-rich-header/84348/); [VB2016](https://www.virusbulletin.com/virusbulletin/2016/11/vb2016-paper-wave-your-false-flags-deception-tactics-muddying-attribution-targeted-attacks/)).
- **"Shared tools/infrastructure = same actor."** Turla used OilRig's own tools and C2 ([CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2019/10/21/nsa-and-ncsc-release-joint-advisory-turla-group-activity)).
- **"Vault 7 proves the CIA framed Russia/China."** Marble shows an obfuscation capability, not a documented operation ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2017/03/31/wikileaks-exposes-cia-anti-forensics-tool-that-makes-uncle-sam-seem-fluent-in-enemy-tongues/567104)).
- **"Naming = arrest."** Khoroshev, the GRU officers and the Sandworm officers are named and indicted, but at large.
